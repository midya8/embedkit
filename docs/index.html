This is not a softmax. Sigmoid scores distinguish sampled data/noise events. They are not calibrated word probabilities. The original trainer updates one event at a time, using a ±15 logit clamp. A frozen summed-gradient step is a different operation.

A / EXPERIMENT DESK

Strict JSON. Dimensions 1-16, output rows 1-12, events 1-33, |coordinate| ≤ 100. Duplicate keys, unknown fields and nonfinite values are rejected. Input edits clear old results immediately.

The synthetic topics are separated by construction. Neighbor purity is a fixture regression, not evidence of natural-language embedding quality. The original trainer excludes the positive context and already drawn negatives, caps negatives at vocabulary size minus one, and falls back to the first eligible word after 20 rejected draws. Its base unigram^0.75 weights therefore do not equal its final sequential draw law.


E / CONTRACT & ATTRIBUTION
Small enough to inspect.
Honest enough to reproduce.
Twelve fixtures capture 72 real event calls from the unchanged trainer's first twelve positive pairs on the complete corpus, using its real RNG and sampler. Seven constructed controls are explicitly separate: repeated negative, conflicting positive/negative row, zero outputs, saturation, overshoot, one row, and maximum shape.
Repeated negatives and conflicting labels are allowed editor controls, not claims about the original trainer's sampler. Center and output tables are distinct even when the word text is the same. This inspector does not estimate an expected sampling objective, train a language model, or assert embedding quality improves after one step.
SGNS and its gradients are standard methods, not a new algorithm. Stable softplus avoids overflow; it does not make floating arithmetic exact. Original training uses the current Python runtime's sum behavior; the inspector explicitly uses sequential binary64 reductions. Cross-runtime comparisons use declared numeric tolerances.
